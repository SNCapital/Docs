    ADR-0004: bounded dual-market collection¶

    Status: accepted for the staged paper rollout on 2026-09-29. This decision supersedes ADR-0002 INV-UNIVERSE-1's full-catalogue collection requirement.

    One collector process owns both spot and USD-M ingress and one D9 publisher. Subjects remain md.spot.* and md.usdm.*; the archiver consumes both and stores a market column on every new row. Earlier paper staging segments were USD-M only; merge labels their missing market field usdm without altering those preserved source files. Spot depth uses the venue-supported <symbol>@depth stream at its default 1000 ms cadence. USD-M depth keeps <symbol>@depth@500ms. Binance Spot lists only the default 1000 ms and optional @100ms diff-depth cadences; using @500ms for Spot would leave the new Spot market without depth updates. See the Spot diff-depth stream contract. Calc and the paper trader remain USD-M only. They read the same persisted USD-M selection as the collector, so a smaller collection set cannot silently turn uncollected futures into expected live bars. Existing completed bar history, strategy history, positions, source archives and old volumes are retained.

    collection_top_n is required, defaults to 100 in live_config.yaml, and accepts any positive integer. It is applied independently to each market. The eligible universe is actively trading USDT quote pairs; USD-M also requires PERPETUAL. Restricting the quote to USDT makes every score a sum of the same quote unit and avoids unverified conversions for USDC, FDUSD and other quote assets. A symbol needs all 30 completed UTC daily klines. The score is the sum of the venue's daily quote-volume field over [UTC day -30, UTC day), sorted descending with symbol ascending for exact ties. Incomplete new listings remain outside the ranking until their 30-day window is complete; absence is explicit, never filled with zero or a 24-hour ticker.

    The collector refreshes after the next completed UTC day, using one bounded daily-kline request per eligible pair, a 250 ms inter-request pace and its shared 600-weight/minute collector quota. Both markets share that limiter, including Retry-After and an IP ban pause. A refresh makes one pass. A transient failure keeps the validated last-good selection and logs its cutoff as stale; without a valid cache, startup fails. An atomic, fsynced, checksum-validated cache in a persistent shared volume carries market, N, cutoff and ordered symbols. Cache filenames bind N and the explicit retained-symbol list; a configuration change starts a new acquisition without overwriting earlier rankings. Log entries expose the effective list, count, cutoff and stale state.

    BTCUSDT is the bounded benchmark exception when active but below rank N. collection_retained_symbols provides at most 20 unique uppercase USDT symbols for existing positions or required benchmarks. The collector adds only active eligible exceptions. The paper trader checks every held symbol is in the selected list before live subscription. Its historical rows are not removed when a symbol leaves the live selection. Operational configuration must list held symbols before the staged start; a missing held symbol fails the paper startup instead of silently losing live marking.

    The shared ranking volume is written by collector and mounted read-only by calc and trader. The production process creates the cache directory and files on first successful acquisition. A corrupt or mismatched cache fails loud. Daily selection changes replace only the affected market's WS generation; calc applies a changed USD-M set at its next boundary, preserving its existing per-symbol completion and verdict logic. The old MD stream and imported PG history are never truncated to implement this decision. Recovery scans may encounter old full-catalogue events; only selected symbols enter new calc engines, and the scalar cursor still reflects the consumed stream position.

    Acceptance requires both markets progressing together, the first funding cycle, stable D9 and archiver queues, per-symbol calc verdicts, fresh settled bars and paper reconciliation before the 24-hour soak starts. The legacy spot writer stays live until new spot readiness is proven, then is stopped with an exact cutover and gap record. A 24-hour soak is started only after those gates.

    Live top-100-per-market ingress exposed a trade archive throughput limit from one-message JetStream pulls. The trade durable therefore requests up to 16 messages per request, below the client pending bound so the subscription queue retains delivery and protocol headroom. nats-py may return a prefilled queue with more than 16 messages; the archiver processes every result within the applicable bounded pending count. After an empty or partial fetch, it waits for the server to report no outstanding pull request before requesting another batch. Full batches avoid that extra server query. Live retained-backlog trials showed that this wait alone does not prevent slow-consumer overflow during a segment close. The trade subscription uses a 4,096-message and 8 MiB local pending bound; other archive kinds keep the 64-message bound. At the observed roughly 1,004 trade messages per second, the count provides about four seconds of receive headroom. The payload byte bound and the 768 MiB archiver container limit remain enforced. The existing trade durable keeps its max_waiting=512: NATS 2.10.29 rejects changing that setting in place, so the archiver validates it rather than recreating the durable or discarding its ACK position. The staged live queue and memory gate must confirm that this bounded receive budget is sufficient. Low-rate archive subjects retain their single-message pull. Fetch batching does not advance acknowledgement: every row remains unacknowledged until its Parquet segment is closed and fsynced. The staged gate measures the live trade pending slope and archive close and readback after this change; a synthetic selected-load readback alone is not sufficient evidence of sustainable live operation.

    The first cursorless calc boot uses its single ordered md.usdm.> consumer to replay the retained stream for cold selected symbols. It does not create a second per-symbol historical consumer after the drain. The completion pass still assigns a verdict before any boundary or live bar is released. A cold symbol added after a saved shard cursor, or a corrupt checkpoint diff leg, uses a bounded batch iterator and closes its temporary consumer when done. The scalar cursor continues to represent the consumed stream position; the selected-symbol filter applies before engine intake. Existing PG bars and raw source remain intact, including intervals the retained MD stream cannot cover. Those intervals require explicit gap evidence, not invented bars.

    The staged first boot consumed 7,517,687 USD-M messages and reached its drain barrier on 2026-09-29 at 15:30:26 UTC. Three seconds later NATS 2.10.29 was OOM-killed at its former 1 GiB container limit, with 1,034,760 KiB anonymous RSS in the kernel record. The redundant per-symbol replay and unbounded Python result list were removed before retry; the record does not prove which allocation inside NATS triggered the final spike. The broker remains pinned to 2.10.29 with sync_always. Its container limit is 1,536 MiB for the measured cold replay. All nine paper Compose service limits then total 13,184 MiB; adding the operator's 2,048 MiB limit stays below the host's 15,949 MiB physical RAM. The new limit is a fault-driven budget, and the next live trial must demonstrate no OOM, bounded queue, and fresh bars before paper execution.

    The next cold calc retry was stopped safely on 2026-09-29 at 16:12:55 UTC after its memory rose from about 400 MiB to 1.86 GiB while the retained USD-M stream still had 9.49 million messages pending. It had not produced a recovery verdict or fresh bar. Presence stamps retained distinct event seconds in up to three Python sets per symbol and 30-minute window until the drain barrier. An anchored integer bitmap per window now retains exact distinct-second counts, including out-of-order arrivals and accumulator merges. Cold pre-tip messages do not retain per-message stamp IDs; the separate live deduplicator remains bounded. The next staged replay must measure calc memory and complete all verdict, bar, queue and archive gates.

    The bitmap reduced the early slope but the 16:31:50 UTC retry still reached about 1.94 GiB RSS with more than 10 million MD messages pending, so calc was again stopped cleanly before its limit. The retained stream covers about 18 hours: at most about 3,600 dense 791-key rows for 100 symbols, which cannot alone explain that RSS. The NATS Python client's 512 MiB pending-byte limit counts payload bytes without Python message and header objects; its actual resident queue can be materially larger. The exact contribution from that queue, bounded engine windows, message-ID deduplication and captured rows is not yet measured. Low-rate drain diagnostics now report each retained count and current RSS. The next guarded replay is a measurement gate, not acceptance of the current memory trend.

    The 17:04 UTC guarded retry isolated another cold-replay retention path: while the NATS client queue was nearly empty and engine slots had reached their fixed plateau, bootstrapping books held 62,635 depth diffs and kept growing. A book with a known snapshot update ID now rejects incoming stale diffs before buffering. After a failed snapshot bridge it discards already buffered stale diffs while preserving the future bridge chain, including the USD-M equal-ID bridge. Focused red/green tests cover these cases. The next replay must still prove memory plateaus and every selected symbol gets a recovery verdict; code-level regression coverage alone is insufficient.

    The live archiver stopped at 17:39 UTC because nats-py surfaced an idle pull as asyncio.TimeoutError, while the service only handled the library-specific NatsTimeoutError. Both specific timeout forms now enter the existing server-quiescence wait before retry. Unexpected fetch errors still propagate. A focused red/green regression covers the actual asyncio exception. The archiver must be rebuilt and observed catching up from its durable ACK state before paper readiness is claimed.

    The broker was OOM-killed again at 18:21 UTC with 1,560,212 KiB anonymous RSS at its 1,536 MiB cgroup limit while calc was stopped. After preserving the same JetStream volume, a restart with collector and archiver attached was OOM-killed again at 18:28 UTC with 1,568,168 KiB anonymous RSS. The two clients were then stopped and the broker recovered its 22,626,713 retained MD messages; with zero client connections its RSS was about 436 MiB. This separates the recurring broker fault from calc's cold replay. Do not represent the current 1,536 MiB budget or top-100 selection as a proven stable production envelope. Isolate client load, fix the measured cause or set a justified bounded budget, then repeat the dual-market and 24-hour soak gates. The interrupted collection intervals are real gaps.
