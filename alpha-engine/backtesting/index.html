    Backtesting & PnL¶

    How AlphaPipeline.run() turns target weights into a PnL series, and what the cost model captures (and doesn't).

    PnL definition¶

    Implemented in compute_pnl (alpha_engine/backtest/pnl.py), re-exported from alpha_engine.pipeline for backwards compatibility. The per-bar math is pnl_core in the same module — the one kernel; do not implement PnL math anywhere else.

    For each bar \(t\) (close-fill mode; vwap mode shifts weights by 2 and earns VWAP returns instead):

    \[ \begin{aligned} r_{i,t} &= \frac{p_{i,t} - p_{i,t-1}}{p_{i,t-1}} \\ \text{gross}_t &= \sum_i w_{i, t-1} \cdot r_{i, t} \\ \text{fees}_t &= \sum_i \big| w_{i,t} - w^{\text{acc}}_{i,t} \big| \cdot \text{fee\_rate} \\ \text{funding}_t &= -\sum_i \text{funding}_{i,t} \cdot w_{i,t-1} \\ \text{net}_t &= \text{gross}_t \;-\; \text{fees}_t \;+\; \text{funding}_t \end{aligned} \]

    where the accounting weight \(w^{\text{acc}}\) is the previous weight passively carried by the price move:

    \[ w^{\text{acc}}_{i,t} \;=\; w_{i,t-1} \cdot \frac{p_{i,t}}{p_{i,t-1}} \]

    So \(|w_t - w^{\text{acc}}_t|\) is the traded notional between bars — the part that incurs a fee. Pure price drift on a held position costs nothing.

    The shift-by-1 on weights matters: at time \(t\) you're using the position you took at \(t-1\), so you don't see your own decision affect the price.

    Why subtract funding?¶

    Sign convention on USD-M perpetuals: positive funding ⇒ longs pay shorts. If you're long (\(w > 0\)) and funding is positive, you pay, and your PnL goes down. The formula encodes that as pnl -= funding · w.

    If funding is None (e.g. spot-only backtest) the funding leg is zero.

    Cost model — what's in, what's out¶

    alpha_engine/backtest/pnl.py::pnl_core:

    gross  = (w.shift(k) * ret).sum(axis=1)          # k=1 close, k=2 vwap
traded = (w - w.shift(1) * price_change).abs()   # net of passive drift
costs  = (traded * cost_rate).sum(axis=1)        # fee + spread/2 + slip
    Captured Not captured
    Round-trip exchange fee at 0.00016 (= 1.6 bps maker on Binance USD-M) Market impact (size-dependent)
    Half quoted spread per trade (ob__minor__spread_bps__mean, fallback spread_default_bps) Borrow cost on shorts
    Flat slippage via slippage_bps Currency / FX (n/a — USD-quoted)
    Per-bar rebalance cost via change in target
    Funding settlement effect

    Knobs: compute_pnl(fee_rate=…, slippage_bps=…, spread_default_bps=…), exposed on the CLI as --fee-bps / --slippage-bps / --spread-default-bps.

    Daily turnover¶

    backtest/turnover.py::daily_turnover integrates |Δw| per ticker per day, then sums across tickers. It's reported in summary() as mean_daily_turnover and median_daily_turnover.

    A daily turnover of 1.0 means you completely re-trade your gross exposure each day. Turnover × fee_rate is a useful sanity check on the PnL gross-vs-net gap.

    Sharpe annualisation¶

    daily   = pnl_series.resample("1D").sum()
sharpe  = (daily.mean() / daily.std()) * sqrt(365)

    We resample bar-level PnL to daily before computing mean / std. This is the right choice when the bar cadence differs (e.g. comparing 30-min strategies to a hypothetical 1-hour strategy). Use \(\sqrt{365}\) — crypto trades 24/7.

    If you want bar-level Sharpe, compute it directly from the pnl_gross / pnl_net series: sharpe_bar = pnl.mean() / pnl.std() * sqrt(48 * 365).

    Drawdown¶

    \[ \text{dd}_t \;=\; \text{cum}_t \;-\; \max_{s \le t}\, \text{cum}_s \]

    Reported as max_drawdown (the most negative value) per leg in the summary. It's an additive PnL drawdown, not a percentage — the unit is "fraction of notional", same as pnl_gross.

    Plots¶

    fig = pipeline.plot("dashboard")     # 6-panel grid
pipeline.plot("pnl")                  # cumulative gross + net
pipeline.plot("drawdown")             # cum_max − cum
pipeline.plot("weights")              # heatmap (timestamps × tickers)
pipeline.plot("turnover")             # daily turnover bars
pipeline.plot("allocations")          # per-strategy weight over time

    Implementations in alpha_engine/backtest/plots.py. Each returns a matplotlib Figure; pipeline.plot() doesn't call plt.show() itself — that's the caller's job (the CLI does it for you when not --no-plot).

    Summary dict¶

    After pipeline.run():

    pipeline.summary()
# {
#   "n_strategies":          int,
#   "strategy_names":        [str, …],
#   "n_tickers":             int,
#   "n_timestamps":          int,
#   "date_range":            "...",
#   "mean_daily_turnover":   float,
#   "median_daily_turnover": float,
#   "gross": {"total_pnl": ..., "annualized_sharpe": ..., "max_drawdown": ...},
#   "net":   {"total_pnl": ..., "annualized_sharpe": ..., "max_drawdown": ...},
# }

    The gross / net split is the single most important diagnostic. If gross.annualized_sharpe > 0 and net.annualized_sharpe < 0, the strategy is producing alpha but burning it on fees — reduce turnover (longer EMA, larger rebalance cadence) before searching for a better signal.

    Common results to watch¶

    • Net Sharpe negative, gross positive → fees / funding eat the alpha. Lower turnover or trade larger names with tighter spreads.
    • Net Sharpe positive, max drawdown larger than realistic risk budget → tail risk. Check the drawdown plot, look at concentration (hh_buy, hh_sell metrics).
    • Allocations fully pinned at max_strategy_weight → at least one strategy is dominating. Either constraints are too lax, or the others contribute nothing — drop them.
    • Allocations bounce wildly between bars → lookback is too short or the strategies are too correlated. See Markowitz.
