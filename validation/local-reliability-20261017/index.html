    Local reliability stand recovery, October 17¶

    Scope is the local backend-live project. Trader and dashboard are intentionally absent. The remote kafka-candidate production stack was not modified.

    Findings¶

    Calc stopped advancing raw offsets on October 13. A native diagnostic showed all eight Spot partitions attached to the internal broker with no leader; the client had no real brokers and an empty metadata hint despite configured rebootstrap. Its consume calls continued returning, and cached high offsets made reported lag zero. The last complete bar was October 13 at 03:00 UTC; the last nonempty bar was 03:30. The internal reason librdkafka lost and failed to rediscover its brokers was not established; the fix detects and terminates this failure rather than claiming to repair the library.

    The archiver stalled on October 14 at 14:09:10 UTC after saving its local ledger. Kafka unloaded the coordinator partitions at 14:09:10.711 and reloaded them with the next epoch at 14:09:12.989. All 16 broker commits remained one batch behind the local ledger. Its native stack was blocked in rd_kafka_commit_queue from Consumer_commit. Recovery never reads these broker commits, so the blocking duplicate write was removed.

    S3 credentials were absent. Validation occurred only when daily merging first needed the sink, allowing green health while every daily iteration failed. The sink is now initialized before Kafka or health startup.

    The broker/controller trigger is also recorded. Controller events reached 25,708.86 ms around the calc failure and 27,555.47 ms around the archive failure. On October 13 it fenced the only broker at 03:08:45.908 and again 03:08:58.698; on October 14 it fenced it at 14:09:07.155. Both incidents therefore removed partition leaders and coordinators before the client faults.

    Kafka has a 1.5 GiB container limit and a 512 MiB heap. Its cgroup reports memory-limit pressure, swap use and substantial page-cache refaults. These are resource-pressure evidence, not proof of a particular historical I/O or scheduler cause. GC pauses in the three-minute failure windows peaked at 19 ms and 151 ms, so they do not account for the 25-28 second controller latency. Historical host I/O and scheduling telemetry was not available. The local Compose override raises broker memory to 3 GiB and total memory/swap allowance to 4 GiB. The same limits were applied live with docker update; the broker was not restarted, its heap was not changed, and the pre-existing GC log was preserved. This reduces observed resource pressure without claiming to establish the historical 25-28 second pause mechanism.

    Recovery and retained evidence¶

    Private operational artifacts are under data/reliability-20261017/. They include container identities and mounts, two PostgreSQL dumps, original archive ledger, exact Kafka bounds, 24 expired ranges, and both new generation records. The database dump taken after stopping the two consumers is the recovery copy.

    The old calc generation, symbol state and empty outbox tables were moved to reliability_before_20261017. Completed bar history remains in public with its original payloads. All 26,200 original primary keys, gap flags and payload bytes were compared against the stopped database dump and matched exactly. The new calc generation starts at the recorded current high offsets; engines and books are cold. Available earlier raw records were not substituted into closed historical bars. Historical data loss and the deliberate generation boundary remain explicit.

    The old backend_archiver_staging volume remains attached only to the finite backend-reliability-archive-flush job. The production merge, upload, verify and purge functions process its three closed days. The new archiver uses backend_reliability_archiver_staging and starts at the recorded earliest retained offsets for both raw topics. It can catch up independently of the old merge. Both roots use the same local bucket, with disjoint day ranges.

    The first retained raw messages in the pre-recovery probe ranged from October 16 at 13:46 UTC to 17:03 UTC across the 16 partitions. Original raw retention was 24 hours with a 10 GiB guard per partition. The raw logs occupied roughly 15.9 GiB logically (17 GiB allocated including broker overhead), with Spot about 4 GiB and USD-M about 12 GiB. This is the segment-granular retained suffix, not an exact byte count for a strictly bounded 24-hour interval.

    During earliest-offset archive recovery, rolling retention advanced Spot partition 7 from below its processed offset to 16,029,016. The service correctly failed closed at next offset 15,758,152. At 17:23 UTC raw retention was raised to 48 hours in both live_config.yaml and the two topic configs. Only the expired 270,864 offsets in that partition were explicitly skipped after preserving the ledger boundary; all other processed offsets and staged files were retained. This additional loss is recorded in pre-recovery/retention-during-recovery.json, not hidden by an automatic reset.

    Local S3¶

    docker-compose.reliability.yml adds SeaweedFS 4.48 pinned by image digest, with a persistent backend_reliability_s3_data volume and no host ports. The server creates backend-reliability idempotently on startup. Generated credentials live in private .env.reliability, excluded from Git and Docker build contexts. All other local storage wiring is in the Compose override.

    The stand is managed with:

    docker compose --project-name backend-live --env-file .env --env-file .env.reliability -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.local-ports.yml -f docker-compose.reliability.yml ps
docker compose --project-name backend-live --env-file .env --env-file .env.reliability -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.local-ports.yml -f docker-compose.reliability.yml up -d --no-deps --no-build calc archiver local-s3

    The override uses the locally built backend-live:reliability-20261017 image. The build starts from the recorded existing backend image and copies only the three patched runtime modules. Build logs and installed image identities are retained with the operational evidence.

    tools/flush_archive.py owns only the offline command surface. It calls the production archive functions and fails if unverified merged objects remain. Run it only against a staging root whose ordinary archiver is stopped.

    Observed recovery progress¶

    At 17:27 UTC, archiver processed about 2,650 rows/s over an 86-second sample. Incoming messages accounted for about 914/s, leaving net catch-up of 1,737/s. Its retained backlog decreased from 66,680,856 to 66,531,239 messages. At that sample rate the remaining replay is roughly 10.6 hours; this is an estimate, not a completion guarantee. Retention must cover outage plus replay time.

    The old staging root occupied 38 GiB before the large October 12 trade upload was verified. That upload contained 26,243,121 rows; the inputs were removed only after verification. The disk then had about 33 GiB free. Local S3 and staging still share the same host disk, so moving bytes is not a separate capacity reserve.

    The finite flush job inherits the application image's healthcheck, but does not serve HTTP. Its inherited unhealthy status is unrelated to merge progress; use job exit status and upload verification logs for this one-shot container. Calc and the ordinary archiver have actual healthy endpoints.

    At the 17:30 UTC boundary, PostgreSQL received 100 bars for 100 symbols, all with nonempty metric keys. The durable output outbox drained to zero after publication. All 100 bars are correctly incomplete because the generation started cold at 17:12; normal warm-up and book snapshot bootstrap still apply. Calc, ordinary archiver and S3 reported healthy, with zero restarts for the current containers. This is live repair evidence, not a completed soak.

    Verification¶

    • Calc regressions fail without the monitor and pass with it, both during initial drain and live following. Coverage includes stale cached zero lag, expired offsets, advancing consumers, quiet partitions, concurrent progress, and a partition paused while a probe is in flight.
    • The archive consume regression processes two durable batches without a coordinator commit, probes uncached bounds and resumes from the exact ledger.
    • Missing credentials fail before Kafka and health startup.
    • The unit gate passed 2403 tests, with nine existing skips and one expected failure. The local dashboard credentials are cleared only in the test process because one dashboard test otherwise receives authenticated responses.
    • Kafka/PostgreSQL integration passed nine tests against isolated topics and schemas. The sequential slow/load gate passed all 23 tests in 498 seconds.
    • The local S3 integration passed both 1 MiB and 65 MiB objects through the production single-part and multipart paths, idempotent retry, full GET SHA-256 comparison and verified staging deletion. Its bucket was created by server startup, not by the test.
    • A separate object survived an actual S3 container restart with matching SHA-256. The test object was then removed.

    These checks do not establish a new 24-hour reliability or capacity pass. The old observation failed. The local object store shares the host filesystem; its historical archive has no automatic expiration policy. Disk reserve and the catch-up/merge lifecycle must be measured before another week-long soak.

    Disk expansion and 72-hour retention¶

    The virtual disk was extended to 452 GiB on October 17. Its last partition remained at 250 GiB, so /dev/sda3, its LVM physical volume, the ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv logical volume and the mounted ext4 filesystem were expanded online. The partition starts, identities and boot partitions were preserved. The logical volume is now 450 GiB and the reported filesystem capacity is 443 GiB, with approximately 217 GiB available after expansion. Partition-table and LVM metadata backups are in the private operational disk-expansion/ directory. Host LVM operations through the maintenance container require the host IPC namespace for udev cookie completion.

    Raw retention was then raised from the temporary 48 hours to 72 hours (259200000 ms) in the checked-in configuration and both raw topic configs. The production ensure_topics check confirmed the desired configuration and the original topic identities. The 10 GiB guard per partition remains in force, so unusually high traffic can still expire records before 72 hours. No application or broker restart was required; the live services remained healthy and archive catch-up continued.

    Follow-up diagnosis and extended logging¶

    The Kafka 4.2.2 source shows that the slow controller write-event duration includes deferred completion while waiting for metadata log commitment. Those 25-28 second measurements do not prove that the JVM or its controller thread stopped running for the whole interval. GC and heartbeat-thread logs continued during the October 14 episode. The source of the metadata-operation latency remains unproven.

    Both kernel windows and the full system journal were inspected around the failures. No OOM kill, reported block-device error or clock jump was found in those windows. Three unrelated services repeatedly failed to execute their startup commands, producing roughly one thousand failures per 15 minutes. This is host noise, not an established Kafka cause. Raw system journals remain private operational artifacts and are not included here.

    The local Compose override gives Kafka a separate persistent diagnostics volume, prepared by an idempotent initialization service before broker boot. Diagnostic directories must stay outside Kafka topic-data directories. The initializer installs the readable Log4j configuration for the broker user.

    Server and controller logs rotate at 32 MiB with 16 archives each. File appenders use bounded asynchronous queues and console error routing. Raft commit progress and broker heartbeats use DEBUG; the root logger stays INFO. GC and safepoint logs rotate at 32 MiB with eight archives. Continuous JFR uses the default low-overhead settings, a 72-hour age limit and a 512 MiB size limit. Default events include file-force, file-read, file-write and lock waits above 20 ms, plus process and machine CPU samples. These bounds limit each stream; they do not promise 72 hours of every text log.

    The files are under /opt/kafka/logs in backend_reliability_kafka_logs. The pinned runtime records valid JFR chunks, but its ordinary JFR.dump export returned an I/O error and an empty file in both the live and isolated broker. The deployment therefore preserves the repository instead of relying on that export. After an incident, preserve the repository chunks alongside timestamp-matched controller, JVM and system logs; assemble a completed repository with jfr assemble <repository-directory> <incident.jfr>. The 512 MiB limit applies to the current recording. Preserved repositories from previous JVM runs require separate collection and cleanup; they are not covered by that per-recording limit. Default recording settings can contain host/runtime details; keep captures private. A controlled Kafka stop/restart is still distinct from reproducing the original prolonged controller delay.

    The first assembled probe recording contained 84 slow file-force events. The slowest force of the Kafka metadata log took 7.266 seconds on the Raft I/O thread. A synchronous controller-log write took 4.062 seconds. This establishes current I/O delays on the shared disk during recovery, not the precise cause of the historical 25-28 second episodes. It also justifies keeping the added diagnostic file logging off Kafka's critical threads. The assembled recording was readable with the JDK jfr summary tool and included CPU, file-force and thread-wait events.

    The healthcheck explicitly removes diagnostic JVM options before invoking the Kafka CLI. Otherwise each poll starts another recording, rotates the broker JVM log and leaves an extra preserved repository. The integration probe verifies that an actual healthcheck leaves the repository list unchanged.

    During a later recovery sample, the guest reported 105.2 ms mean write latency, about 18.8 outstanding I/O operations and 25.3 percent CPU I/O wait. A repeated unit run on the shared filesystem timed out in fsync and other filesystem operations. With temporary unit fixtures on tmpfs, all 2382 tests passed without increasing their five-second timeout. This isolates that run's storage interference from the unit contracts; it is not a durability or disk performance pass.

    The final rollout recreated the broker with the same data volume and topic identities. Calc recovered with replayed verdicts and the archive ledger advanced by 1,658,879 messages relative to the pre-rollout sample, without resetting any offset. During logging rollout and shared-disk overload, calc and archiver restarted automatically; this is recovery evidence rather than a claim of uninterrupted operation. All three services returned healthy and the old finite archive job was resumed after a temporary diagnostic pause.

    The isolated PR was rebuilt directly on main, excluding the independent event-metadata/book-snapshot commits. On that head, 2383 unit cases passed across the full tmpfs run and isolated retries of two timing-sensitive cases during broker startup. Fourteen Kafka/PostgreSQL/S3 integration tests and the separate empty-volume diagnostics integration passed. The initial shared-disk full unit run had 64 failures concentrated in fsync and other filesystem waits; those negative results remain part of the storage diagnosis.
